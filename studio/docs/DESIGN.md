# Design principles and reference study

Learn mechanisms, not another creator's identity. Inspect available references with permission. If full playback is unavailable, record exact sampled frames and state that limitation. Links/thumbnail/catalog counts are not videos watched.

Study hook, information density, shot rhythm, reading intervals, scale/density contrast, typography, material, handoffs, camera purpose, sound hierarchy and resolution. Translate observations into original decisions in the project's brief. Do not require a reference if none is needed or invent a study that never ran.

A film should communicate one idea even when muted. At each important moment one focal action must be clear. Supporting detail should serve the message. A camera move must reveal a relationship, not just prove a camera exists. Build depth with overlap, occlusion, scale, projection and shading before expensive 3D.

Choose motion verbs first (fold, split, stack, reveal, align, resolve), then per-property easing and timing. Design anticipation/contact/follow-through where meaningful. An impact on every beat removes hierarchy. Avoid identical stagger waves, eight equal demo sections, generic gradients and the same wipe between unrelated ideas.

Keep type readable at small display size. Use real font metrics and inspect counters, masks and stroke expansion. Move essential copy into a readable hold. Intentional display cropping is different from accidentally cutting information. Do not shrink a whole script into decorative microtext.

Keep supplied marks unchanged unless asked; don't generate a replacement logo. Never borrow a reference creator's logo, footage, track, palette identity or exact sequence merely because its code/catalog is public.

Existing user preference: strong energy and new visual ideas when appropriate; no hissing sweep transitions or unwanted persistent black caption bar. A new brief can override those defaults. Calm/quiet/stillness remain valid when they serve the message.
