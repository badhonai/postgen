# Direction before effects

Read AGENTS.md, docs/WORKFLOW.md, docs/DESIGN.md and the active project brief. Research/setup requests do not authorize production. When production is authorized, use the actual message/audience/format/duration/voice; don't default every job to a 15-second reel.

1. Produce three distinct concept candidates—not palettes. Each needs a one-sentence story, visual mechanism, signature payoff, reference principle and feasibility.
2. Recommend a dominant coherence model: editorial system, persistent world/object or material evolution may be useful. None is mandatory. Montage can be coherent; a single object/camera is optional.
3. Describe the film without “premium”, “cinematic” or lists of effects. If the idea is only effects, it is unfinished.
4. Define palette roles, type hierarchy, grid/safe areas, density, material rules, motion register and sound role. Typeface selection must fit the brief, not an arbitrary community ban/list.
5. Outline hook, development, contrast, payoff and resolution. Durations need not be equal. Avoid equal capability-demo sections and repeat-wipe storytelling.
6. Design the first-second event deliberately. Do not imitate a creator's dark lead-in, exact layout or signature sequence just because it performed well.
7. Keep a rejection log and originality check against recent projects. Every new film needs content-specific ideas, not a recolored template.
8. Design strong static states before animation. Remove a technique if doing so leaves the story unchanged.

Decision tests: readable muted story; one focal action; meaningful payoff; purposeful detail; pacing contrast; truthful identity/facts; original composition and sequence.

Selective basis: LottieFiles staging/emotional intent, Mort1d direction, DeanSPN originality/treatment and prior reference studies. Immutable pointers and license status are in docs/research/source-index.json. These heuristics do not guarantee professional quality or prove how any creator made a reference.
